## Executive summary

**The extracted candidate library is not where Presence's options should come from.**

That is the central finding, and it is not the answer the ingestion work was aiming at. Of 118
extracted component candidates, **4 are worth exposing as options**. Of 8 room kits, **2 are
plausible product options**, both blocked on specific work. Meanwhile the procedural component
registry — built in parallel and now standing at 22 registered components — already covers
almost every authoring role the candidate library cannot fill.

The reason is structural, not a failure of the pipeline. The ingested collection is a set of
**interiors** — a church, a coffee shop, two living rooms, a spaceship, two baked VR rooms.
It was never a set of Presence fixtures. So it contains no rack, no frame, no projection
surface, no archive furniture: precisely the components a spatial publishing platform needs
most. The pipeline extracted faithfully; the source simply did not contain them.

What the candidate library *does* provide is narrow and genuinely valuable: a handful of
sculptural objects with character that procedural primitives would not naturally produce — a
7.8 KB stone plinth, a cloth drape, a set of stacked display blocks — plus a body of
**proportional reference** (ribbed wall rhythm, arcade bay spacing, Gothic arch profile) that
is far more useful reproduced procedurally than shipped as meshes.

**Recommendation:** build the first palette procedurally, treat the candidate library as a
character library and a reference set, and put real effort into the impossible-display lane,
which is where Presence stops being a room simulator and becomes spatial publishing.

## What it lacks

Five authoring categories are **completely empty**:

| Missing | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Racks / garment rails | The load-bearing fixture for any fashion or retail presence |
| Frames / poster surfaces | The gallery workhorse; how a visual artist shows anything |
| Projection / media surfaces | How film, video, DJ and campaign work exists at all |
| Archive furniture | `archive` is a supported room type with archival materials and no furniture |
| True wall systems | Only fragments and one branded graphic |

Also absent across the whole library: anything for **audio**. No listening station, no seating
arranged for sound, nothing for DJs, musicians, oral-history archives, radio or podcast makers.
This is the single largest unserved audience.

**Every one of these gaps is already filled procedurally** by the parallel Gate 4 component
quality round: `presence.suspended-rack`, `presence.garment-hanger`, `presence.framed-media`,
`presence.projection-wall`, `presence.display-shelf`, `presence.ribbed-wall`,
`presence.drape-divider`, `presence.text-sign-card`, `presence.light-fixture` and others.

## Recommended option categories

Aligned to the existing `RoomType` enum where possible, so the taxonomy does not fork:

**Rooms:** boutique/showroom · gallery · archive · studio · living/soft-domestic ·
listening room · performance room · industrial/warehouse · projection room · impossible/hybrid

**Components:** walls & dividers · surfaces (tables/islands/plinths) · racks & shelves ·
media surfaces · display primitives · fixtures & lighting · soft furnishing · seating ·
props & decorative

**Non-geometry:** material presets · skins · actions

## Suggested product-facing names

Full grammar in the taxonomy doc. The rule that matters: **name the spatial idea, never the
source or the client.** "Ribbed Concrete Room", not "Brutalist VR Room Baked". "Dark Boutique
Shell", not "Mobstar Room". Machine-generated candidate ids like
`candidate.chair.interior-7-3bc1-013` must never reach an owner's screen — the taxonomy doc
proposes a stable alias layer (`presence.surface.stone-island@0.1.0`) so the product contract
survives re-ingestion and can later be repointed from a candidate mesh to a procedural
implementation without any layout changing.

## Quality risks

1. **Thumbnails are Workbench renders.** They show silhouette, massing and scale. They do not
   show material response, lighting behaviour or interior atmosphere. Every score in these
   documents is capped by that, and none should be read as an art-direction judgement.
2. **Baked lighting.** The two best room kits are single baked meshes. Their light is painted
   into the texture and will not respond to a Presence lighting profile — likely reading flat
   or double-lit. Untested.
3. **Baked branding.** At least one candidate carries third-party identity in its geometry.
   Others from the same source may too. Anything mined from the coffee-shop source needs
   individual visual checking.
4. **Grouped extractions.** Some candidates are several objects sharing one source node. They
   are components in name only.
5. **Single-mesh kits cannot be skinned.** The two best kits have one baked texture, so
   per-client material swapping — the core of the whole component-reference model — is not
   possible on them without re-authoring.
6. **Only 13 of 118 components were reviewed in detail.** The other 105 are triaged by group.
   Given that four of the five best finds were mis-categorised, there are likely more.

## Scale risks

Source models arrive in whatever unit their author used, and the pipeline deliberately
measures rather than rescales.

| Asset | Measured | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `roomkit.living-room-06d0` | 25.1 m ceiling | Implausible; suggested scale 0.127 |
| `roomkit.unknown-interior-a5e1` | 53.7 m long, 12.3 m ceiling | Suggested scale 0.26 |
| `roomkit.unknown-interior-ffd7` | 21.4 m | Plausible for a nave — which is why it must be confirmed |
| `candidate…3bc1-009` (armchair) | 0.38 m footprint | Doll-sized; scale confidence 1/4 |
| `candidate…3bc1-000` (light stand) | 0.86 m tall | Short for a stand; may be collapsed |
| `candidate…3bc1-013` (risers) | 0.49 m tall | Plausible but unconfirmed |

**No candidate should be placed in a room until its scale is confirmed in 3D.** Mixed-scale
objects in one room is the fastest way to make a space feel wrong for reasons a viewer cannot
articulate.

| Declared licence | Sources | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `CC-BY-4.0` | church, Venator, great drawing room | **Attribution travels** with anything published |

| none found | both baked VR rooms, coffee shop | No provenance at all |

Additional points a human must settle:

- The **Venator** source depicts third-party franchise IP regardless of the model's own licence.
  Excluded here on that basis.
- The **best object in the library is a church altar.** De-textured it carries no religious
  signal, but whether that provenance is acceptable framing is a product and cultural decision,
  not a curation default.
- The three sources with **no embedded metadata** have no provenance to reason from at all.

The product owner has stated they will source assets and that licence concerns are cleared
appropriately for internal use. That is recorded as an owner declaration
(`independentLegalVerification: false`) alongside preserved original provenance. **This
document performs no legal verification and makes no legal conclusion.**

## Recommended next implementation priorities

**P0 — make a credible first palette, entirely procedural**

1. **Room Kit container type.** Six proposed shells are "shell + material style + lighting
   profile" triples with no home in the model. This is the single biggest structural gap
   between the current registry and a usable palette.
2. **Option alias layer.** Stable `presence.*` option ids mapping to backing refs, with public
   name, category, description and the two status axes. Unblocks every palette UI.
3. **White Cube Gallery + Blackout Projection Room.** Two shells beyond boutique. Presence
   currently leans hard toward one aesthetic.

**P1 — breadth and honesty**

4. **Display Primitive arrangement contract** (N Pieces → N transforms). The prerequisite for
   every impossible display. Design once, not per-primitive.
5. **Listening Station.** `listening-station` exists as a Surface type with no component. The
   largest unserved audience.
6. **Archive Wall.** Second-largest gap; `archive` is a room type with materials and no furniture.
7. **Scale confirmation pass** on the five flagged candidates, then promote or drop them.

**P2 — the differentiator**

8. **Constellation Archive** as the first impossible display: widest audience, reuses the
   existing `piece-plane` carrier, needs no new material system, degrades to a semantic list.
9. **Second curation pass** over the ~85 unreviewed non-franchise candidates via a thumbnail
   contact sheet.
10. **Baked-lighting compatibility test** on the two single-mesh kits before either is offered.

## The honest bottom line

The ingestion pipeline did its job: it turned 463 MB of unstructured source into a searchable,
budgeted, provenance-tracked candidate library, and it did so repeatably. But the strategic
value it produced is **not a set of shippable options**. It is:

- five genuinely good objects,
- two rooms worth further work,
- a body of proportional reference, and
- a precise, evidence-backed map of what Presence still has to build.

That last item is worth the most. Presence should build its options palette procedurally, use
the candidate library for character where character is scarce, and spend its distinctive effort
on the displays that could not exist in a physical room at all.
