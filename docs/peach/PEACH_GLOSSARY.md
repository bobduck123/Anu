# PEACH Glossary

These terms are working product language for PEACH. They should help implementation, editorial, stewardship, and business decisions. They are not decorative brand copy.

| Term | Short definition | What it is not | Examples | Phase 1 appearance | Facing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orchard | The whole PEACH environment: Fields, Commons, stewards, members, patrons, Yields, and operating practices. | A literal farm metaphor, a homepage category, or a list of clubs. | The PEACH platform; the total body of active and returned Fields; the stewardship model. | Mostly implicit. May appear in internal docs and navigation architecture, not as heavy public metaphor. | Internal-facing; provisional public use. |
| Field | A cultivated area of communal attention opened around a Seed and moved through reading, conversation, research, making, Yield, and Return. | An event, book club, product category, forum, collection page, or generic project page. | "What does a community learn when it studies itself as seriously as institutions study it?" | The primary Phase 1 object, with a public Field page and admin-editable content. | Public-facing. |
| Seed | The central intrigue that opens a Field. It must carry tension, specificity, and reason-for-now. | A topic label, slogan, theme, SEO keyword, or vague prompt. | "What do we inherit from a place after its institutions leave?" | Displayed prominently on the Field page and used to evaluate Vessels, Gatherings, prompts, and Yield. | Public-facing. |
| Soil | The context, history, people, places, conditions, conflicts, and prior materials surrounding the Seed. | Background filler, generic introduction copy, or academic throat-clearing. | Local histories, institutional records, community memory, maps, timelines, social conditions. | A concise public section plus richer steward notes in admin. | Public-facing and internal-facing. |
| Vessel | A source or entry point that helps people enter and tend a Field. | Store inventory, affiliate product, required reading list, or decorative reference. | Books, films, songs, artworks, oral histories, documents, recipes, locations, images, archives. | 3-5 selected Vessels with descriptions, access notes, and rights/source details. | Public-facing. |
| Tending | Structured participation that helps the Field develop through reading, conversation, research, response, and making. | Posting, commenting, reacting, attending passively, or producing engagement metrics. | Responding to a prompt, annotating a source, sharing a memory with consent, joining a research circle. | 2-3 prompts and selected contribution pathways. | Public-facing. |
| Gathering | A moment of collective interpretation, live or asynchronous. | A generic event listing, networking session, webinar, or content drop. | Reading circle, archive walk-through, oral history salon, maker session, recorded conversation. | At least two Gatherings tied directly to the Field's Seed and Yield pathway. | Public-facing. |
| Contribution | A consented submission into a Field that may inform interpretation, making, or Yield. | An unreviewed public post, social comment, extracted story, or anonymous content unit. | A memory, annotated image, short audio reflection, source note, response to a prompt. | Submitted through a structured form or manual intake with consent fields and editorial review. | Public-facing; review is internal. |
| Maker | A person shaping selected Field material into a cultural work. | A generic creator, influencer, vendor, or content producer. | Editor, artist, researcher, designer, writer, archivist, filmmaker, educator. | May be named on the Field page if involved in producing the Phase 1 Yield. | Public-facing. |
| Steward | The person or team responsible for framing, care, moderation, consent, editorial decisions, and Return. | A community manager focused on engagement alone, or an invisible admin. | Field curator, editor, facilitator, cultural worker, host organization lead. | Required for every Field; visible publicly with responsibility and contact path. | Public-facing and internal-facing. |
| Patron | A person or institution supporting access, production, preservation, or Return. | An advertiser, affiliate sponsor, donor with unrestricted extraction rights, or buyer of influence. | Library sponsor, civic funder, school partner, access patron, production patron. | Optional support/revenue block that states what patronage funds. | Public-facing. |
| Yield | The cultural work or works produced by a Field. | A recap post, content dump, merch line, or engagement report. | Zine, essay series, annotated archive, exhibition, publication, podcast episode, teaching kit, map. | One realistic Phase 1 Yield, scoped tightly enough to make well. | Public-facing. |
| Commons | The shared body of returned Yields and cultural inheritance available for future use, teaching, revision, or continuation. | A private content library, marketplace, gated archive, or dumping ground. | Public archive of Field Yields, accessible publication pages, teaching resources, source trails. | A simple returned Field/Yield page or collection can stand in for a fuller Commons. | Public-facing; implementation may be provisional. |
| Return | The act of placing a Yield back into the Commons with consent, attribution, access, and continuity. | Publishing and forgetting, extracting and selling, or putting content behind a permanent wall. | Public release, community handback, archive deposit, teaching version, annotated source list. | Required as a plan at Field launch and executed when the Field reaches Returned status. | Public-facing and internal-facing. |
| Season | A bounded cycle of Field activity with a beginning, active tending period, making period, and Return. | A marketing campaign, event series label, or arbitrary calendar quarter. | A six-week Field; a summer archive season; a three-month commissioned Field. | May be used to define Phase 1 timing and expectations. | Public-facing; still provisional. |

## Usage Rule

Use the smallest number of PEACH terms needed to explain behavior. If a term does not clarify what a participant, steward, maker, or patron can do, use plain language instead.
