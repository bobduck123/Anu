# PEACH Field V1 Specification

## Definition

A Field is the core PEACH launch object. It is a cultivated area of communal attention opened around a Seed of intrigue and moved through Soil, Vessels, Gatherings, Tending, Making, Yield, and Return.

Field V1 is designed for Phase 1: one excellent Field, stewarded carefully, with enough structure to become future product, data, and UI specification.

## Required Field Sections

### 1. Seed

The Seed is the central intrigue or question.

Requirements:

- must be specific enough to exclude many possible Fields;
- must contain tension, contradiction, wound, memory, future, or unresolved significance;
- must have reason-for-now;
- must be answerable through communal interpretation and making, not only expert explanation;
- must point toward a possible Yield.

Non-example: "Community and books."

Better example: "What does a community learn when it studies itself as seriously as institutions study it?"

### 2. Soil

Soil is the context surrounding the Seed.

It may include:

- history;
- people;
- place;
- social conditions;
- institutional context;
- memories;
- prior works;
- unresolved conflicts;
- language and naming;
- current pressures or opportunities.

Soil should help participants understand why the Seed matters and what kinds of attention are appropriate.

### 3. Vessels

Vessels are source materials that help people enter the Field.

Allowed Vessel types include:

- books;
- films;
- songs;
- artworks;
- oral histories;
- documents;
- recipes;
- places;
- images;
- maps;
- archives;
- letters;
- objects;
- recordings;
- essays;
- data or public records.

Vessel requirements:

- title or label;
- type;
- source/creator where known;
- access path;
- rights or usage note where relevant;
- why it belongs in this Field.

Vessels are not store inventory. A book may be a Vessel, but a Field must not become a shopping list.

### 4. Gatherings

Gatherings are moments of collective interpretation.

They may be:

- live;
- digital;
- recorded;
- asynchronous;
- place-based;
- invite-only;
- public;
- member-only;
- facilitated workshops.

Gathering requirements:

- title;
- format;
- date/time or availability window;
- steward/facilitator;
- intended interpretive purpose;
- connection to Seed, Vessels, Tending prompts, or Yield.

### 5. Tending Prompts

Tending prompts are structured invitations to contribute safely and meaningfully.

Prompt requirements:

- clear scope;
- plain consent expectations;
- optionality and safety boundaries;
- contribution format;
- review expectation;
- whether the response may inform the Yield;
- whether anonymous or private submission is allowed.

Prompts should not coerce disclosure. Sensitive prompts require extra care and a non-public path.

### 6. Making Room

Making Room is the editorial and creative space where selected Contributions, Vessels, and interpretations become cultural works.

In Phase 1 this can be manual, but the Field must state:

- who is making;
- what material may be used;
- how selection happens;
- how contributors are credited;
- what the intended Yield is;
- when the Yield is expected;
- how contributors can ask questions or withdraw consent where possible.

### 7. Yield

The Yield is the work or works produced by the Field.

Phase 1 Yields should be realistic and finishable. Examples:

- a short publication;
- a digital zine;
- an annotated source trail;
- an audio essay;
- a small exhibition page;
- a teaching kit;
- a community map;
- a recorded conversation edited with context;
- a Field report with selected contributions and interpretation.

A recap post is not enough unless it is shaped as a usable cultural work.

### 8. Return

Return is how the Yield enters the Commons and remains available for future inheritance, teaching, revision, or continuation.

Return requirements:

- public or community-access location;
- attribution;
- consent scope;
- preservation format;
- source trail where possible;
- teaching/reuse notes where appropriate;
- continuation path, including whether it may seed future Fields.

## Field Statuses

| Status | Meaning | Public behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | Steward is developing the Field. | Not public unless previewed. |
| Upcoming | Field is announced but not yet open for Tending. | Public page can show Seed, Soil, Vessels, Gatherings, support path, and open date. |
| Open | Field is accepting members, registrations, or initial interest. | Public page invites entry and shows upcoming Gatherings. |
| Tending | Field is actively gathering Contributions and interpretation. | Tending prompts and Contribution intake are active. |
| Making | Contribution intake may be closed or limited while makers produce the Yield. | Public page explains what is being made and expected timing. |
| Yielding | Yield is being released, exhibited, taught, performed, or published. | Public page features the Yield and acknowledgements. |
| Returned | Yield has entered the Commons with Return requirements met. | Field page becomes part of the Commons and points to reuse/continuation. |
| Archived/Dormant | Field is no longer active, or is preserved for reference and possible future revival. | Page remains available if rights and consent allow. |

## Public Page Requirements

The public Field page must make the Field legible without turning it into a marketing page.

Required public sections:

- Field title and status.
- Seed.
- Soil.
- Vessels.
- Gatherings.
- Tending prompts or explanation of when Tending opens.
- Making Room or intended Yield.
- Steward.
- Consent and contribution expectations.
- Support path.
- Return plan.

The public page must not lead with a product grid, generic community CTA, social feed, or event calendar detached from the Field.

## Admin And Steward Requirements

Stewards must be able to:

- create and edit Field content;
- change Field status;
- add, edit, reorder, and remove Vessels;
- add, edit, reorder, and remove Gatherings;
- add, edit, pause, and close Tending prompts;
- review Contributions before publication or use;
- record consent scope;
- mark Contributions as private, review-only, selected, declined, withdrawn, or published;
- update Yield and Return information;
- manage attribution;
- update support/revenue copy;
- export or preserve Field material for production and Commons return.

## Contribution And Consent Requirements

Every Contribution must have:

- contributor name or chosen attribution;
- contact path if follow-up is needed;
- prompt or Field association;
- submission content or file reference;
- consent to steward review;
- consent status for possible use in Yield;
- publication visibility preference;
- attribution preference;
- withdrawal/contact instructions;
- date submitted;
- review status.

Consent must be plain-language and specific. A contributor should understand whether their Contribution is:

- only for steward review;
- eligible to be quoted;
- eligible to be edited;
- eligible to be published in the Yield;
- eligible to appear in the Commons;
- attributable by name, pseudonym, organization, or anonymous.

Sensitive material should have a private or restricted pathway.

## Revenue And Support Hooks

Field V1 may support revenue through:

- membership;
- Field patronage;
- access sponsorship;
- commissioned Field inquiry;
- workshop or facilitated program inquiry;
- publication pre-order or purchase where appropriate;
- institutional pack inquiry;
- donation to production, preservation, or public access.

Revenue copy must state what support funds, such as access, stewardship, maker fees, publication, preservation, or Commons Return.

Field V1 must not depend on affiliate or dropship commerce.

## Commons Return Requirements

For a Field to reach Returned status, it must have:

- a completed Yield or clear released artifact;
- an accessible location for the Yield;
- attribution and acknowledgements;
- consent scope honored;
- source/Vessel trail where possible;
- date of Return;
- steward or archive contact path;
- reuse, teaching, revision, or continuation notes;
- relationship to future Fields if relevant.

## Worked Example: Field 001

### Field Metadata

- Field ID: PEACH-FIELD-001
- Working title: Studying Ourselves
- Status: Draft
- Season: Phase 1 pilot season
- Steward: PEACH founding steward
- Maker: Editor/researcher to be appointed
- Access: Public Field page with reviewed contribution intake
- Intended Yield: A short digital Field publication and source trail

### Seed

"What does a community learn when it studies itself as seriously as institutions study it?"

This Seed has tension because institutions often study communities through surveys, policy, markets, risk, demographics, and institutional priorities, while communities themselves are rarely given the tools, time, or editorial structure to study their own memory, contradictions, and future.

Reason-for-now: PEACH is launching as ANU's collective-cultural vertical. Its first Field should demonstrate the act PEACH exists to make repeatable: a community cultivating interest in itself and making something from that attention.

### Soil

Many communities are researched, represented, marketed to, categorized, and reported on by institutions. The resulting knowledge may be useful, but it often sits outside the community's own interpretive life. People inherit summaries of themselves rather than practices for studying themselves.

This Field asks what changes when the tools of attention move closer to the community: when memory, reading, archival fragments, conversation, and making become shared practice rather than external analysis.

Relevant conditions:

- communities are over-measured but under-resourced for self-interpretation;
- cultural memory is fragmented across personal archives, institutional collections, and platform feeds;
- public conversation often rewards performance rather than careful study;
- ANU needs a first PEACH Field that proves culture as cultivation, not content.

### Vessels

1. Vessel: Community self-study question set
   - Type: Original document
   - Access: Published on the Field page
   - Why it belongs: Gives participants a practical way to notice what institutions ask, what they omit, and what the community might ask of itself.
   - Rights note: PEACH-authored, reusable with attribution.

2. Vessel: Local or organizational archive fragment
   - Type: Document, image, newsletter, meeting note, flyer, map, or artifact
   - Access: Submitted or selected by steward with permission
   - Why it belongs: Grounds the Field in specific memory rather than abstract community language.
   - Rights note: Requires source permission before public use.

3. Vessel: Recorded conversation with a community memory-holder
   - Type: Oral history or interview excerpt
   - Access: Edited excerpt or transcript after consent
   - Why it belongs: Brings lived interpretation into the Field without treating memory as raw content.
   - Rights note: Explicit consent required for recording, excerpting, attribution, and Commons Return.

4. Vessel: A public institutional representation of the community
   - Type: Report excerpt, policy text, demographic summary, media article, or public dataset
   - Access: Link or cited excerpt where rights allow
   - Why it belongs: Creates contrast between external study and community self-study.
   - Rights note: Use citation and limited excerpting; link to original source.

5. Vessel: A cultural work made from self-observation
   - Type: Essay, poem, artwork, song, short film, exhibition, or zine
   - Access: Link, excerpt, or viewing/listening note
   - Why it belongs: Shows that studying oneself can become cultural production, not only analysis.
   - Rights note: Respect creator rights and quote/display only with permission or within allowed use.

### Gatherings

1. Gathering: Reading The Outside View
   - Format: Live digital or in-person discussion
   - Purpose: Compare how institutions describe a community with what community members notice, value, dispute, or remember.
   - Connected Vessels: Public institutional representation; archive fragment.
   - Output: Notes for the Field's source trail and possible prompts.

2. Gathering: Making From The Inside View
   - Format: Facilitated maker session
   - Purpose: Select recurring questions, images, phrases, contradictions, and memories from reviewed Contributions and shape the first Yield.
   - Connected Vessels: Oral history excerpt; community self-study question set; selected Contributions.
   - Output: Editorial outline for the Phase 1 Yield.

### Tending Prompts

1. Prompt: "What is one question outsiders often ask about this community, and what question do you wish the community asked itself?"
   - Format: Short written response
   - Safety: Avoid naming private individuals without consent.
   - Consent: Contributor chooses review-only, quote-eligible, or publishable.

2. Prompt: "Share one object, place, phrase, document, image, or memory that helps this community recognize itself."
   - Format: Text plus optional image/file
   - Safety: Contributor must confirm they have permission to share the material.
   - Consent: Separate consent for public display and use in Yield.

3. Prompt: "What has this community learned the hard way that should be preserved for future members?"
   - Format: Written or recorded response
   - Safety: No requirement to disclose trauma; private submission allowed.
   - Consent: Sensitive material defaults to steward review only unless explicitly approved.

### Making Room

The steward and maker review Contributions, Gathering notes, and Vessels to produce a concise digital publication. The publication should not claim to represent the whole community. It should show a method for communal self-study and present selected, consented material as a first Return.

Editorial selection criteria:

- strengthens the Seed;
- respects consent and safety;
- reveals a useful tension, memory, question, or method;
- can be attributed or anonymized according to contributor preference;
- helps future participants continue the Field.

### Phase 1 Yield

Yield: "Studying Ourselves: A First PEACH Field Report"

Format:

- 8-12 page digital publication or equivalent web publication;
- Seed and Soil statement;
- selected Vessels with source trail;
- 6-10 consented excerpts or artifacts;
- short editorial essay;
- method notes for future Fields;
- continuation questions.

This is realistic for Phase 1 because it can be produced manually while proving the full Field lifecycle.

### Support And Revenue Path

Support path:

- founding membership supports access, stewardship, and Commons Return;
- a Field patron may fund maker fees, transcription, publication design, and preservation;
- access sponsorship can cover participation for people or groups who could not otherwise join;
- institutions may inquire about commissioned Fields or facilitated self-study workshops after the pilot.

Revenue boundary:

- no affiliate bookstore;
- no dropship merchandise;
- no sponsor control over conclusions or editorial selection;
- no sale of personal Contributions outside the consented Yield.

### Commons Return Plan

The Yield Returns to the Commons through:

- a public Field page updated to Returned status;
- downloadable or readable digital publication;
- source/Vessel trail with rights-respecting links and notes;
- attribution and acknowledgements;
- consent scope summary;
- teaching/reuse note explaining how another community could adapt the method;
- continuation questions that may become future Seeds.

Field 001 is complete only when the Return is accessible, attributed, consent-honoring, and useful beyond the initial participants.
