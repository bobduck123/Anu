# PEACH Anti-Patterns

Future work must avoid reducing PEACH into a familiar but wrong product category. These anti-patterns are pass/fail checks for concept, product, design, data, and revenue decisions.

| Anti-pattern | Why it is wrong | Do this instead | Pass/fail test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bookstore-first UX | It makes books and transactions the primitive, which collapses PEACH into retail. | Make the Field the primary object. Vessels can include books, but they serve the Seed. | If the homepage's main action is browsing or buying books, fail. |
| Ecommerce grid homepage | A product grid trains users to shop rather than tend a Field. | Lead with the active Field, its Seed, Vessels, Gatherings, Tending prompts, and Yield pathway. | If the first screen looks like Shopify, fail. |
| Affiliate/dropship-first business model | It aligns revenue with conversion volume instead of cultural production. | Use membership, patronage, commissioned Fields, workshops, publications, cultural works, and institutional programs. | If revenue depends mainly on sending users to buy third-party goods, fail. |
| "Book club plus events" framing | It treats reading and events as the product rather than inputs into making and Return. | Frame reading and Gatherings as ways to cultivate a Field and produce a Yield. | If a user can summarize the offer as "read a book, attend a discussion," fail. |
| Generic CMS framing | It turns PEACH into pages, posts, tags, and assets without a cultural production loop. | Model Field, Seed, Soil, Vessels, Gatherings, Tending prompts, Contributions, Yield, and Return. | If the data model can be replaced by "posts and categories" with no loss, fail. |
| Social posting/comment feed as primary interaction | Feeds optimize expression, reaction, and recency over interpretation, care, and making. | Use structured prompts, reviewed Contributions, stewarded Gatherings, and editorial selection. | If unreviewed posts are the main participation mode, fail. |
| Unreviewed public contributions | It risks harm, extraction, spam, misattribution, and low-trust cultural work. | Collect Contributions through moderated intake with consent, review, attribution, and publication decisions. | If a Contribution can appear publicly before review, fail. |
| Extracting community stories without consent | It repeats institutional and platform extraction under cultural language. | Make consent, usage scope, withdrawal/contact path, attribution, and Return explicit. | If a personal story can be reused in a Yield without clear consent, fail. |
| Treating culture as content | It makes culture a feed object optimized for attention capture. | Treat cultural material as inheritance, source, interpretation, contribution, and work. | If success is measured mainly by views, likes, shares, or posting frequency, fail. |
| Membership as discount perks | It trains members to value savings rather than access, stewardship, Commons, and production. | Make membership support participation, access sponsorship, Field production, preservation, and Return. | If the clearest member benefit is discounts, fail. |
| Vague "community" language with no production pathway | It creates sentiment without an operational route from attention to cultural work. | Require every Field to specify Seed, prompts, Gatherings, Contribution review, intended Yield, and Return plan. | If a Field has no plausible Yield and Return, fail. |
| Overuse of agricultural metaphors in a twee/decorative way | It weakens the seriousness of the system and obscures product behavior. | Use PEACH terms only when they clarify roles, objects, lifecycle, or decisions. | If copy relies on cute garden language without operational meaning, fail. |
| Event calendar as product center | Events alone do not create a cultural cultivation system. | Tie Gatherings to Field status, Seed, Vessels, prompts, making, and Return. | If events can exist without a Field, fail for Phase 1. |
| Public archive with no living process | An archive alone can preserve material but does not necessarily cultivate attention or produce work. | Connect sources to Seed, prompts, interpretation, making, and Return. | If the product only stores materials without Tending or Yield, fail. |
| Sponsor capture | Sponsors can distort the Field if they control framing, contribution selection, or Yield conclusions. | Define patron rights narrowly: fund access, production, preservation, or distribution without editorial control. | If a patron can buy conclusions or suppress Contributions, fail. |
| Trauma-mining prompts | Over-intimate prompts can coerce disclosure and turn wounds into material. | Invite meaningful response with safety, optionality, boundaries, and non-public alternatives. | If a prompt pressures vulnerable disclosure for the sake of impact, fail. |
| Commons as private inventory | Return loses meaning if Yields are treated only as paywalled assets. | Balance paid support with a meaningful public or community-access return. | If no usable part of the Yield returns to the Commons, fail. |

## Minimum Concept Test

A PEACH feature passes only if it helps a Field move from Seed to Soil, Vessels, Tending, Gathering, Making, Yield, and Return.

If a future agent can honestly describe the proposed product as "a book club website with a shop and events," the proposal fails.
